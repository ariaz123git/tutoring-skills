# Adaptive hint and step-through design

Use this reference for taught, difficult, or multipart homework items. Match Mastering-style
coaching in function: optional part-level hints, smaller Socratic questions, and targeted
feedback. Do not imitate branding or impose a fixed number of reveals.

## Decision sequence

For each part, identify the decisions a capable solver must make. Add a hint only for a
decision that is likely to block the learner.

1. **No hint** for direct retrieval or routine execution the learner is meant to do cold.
   If the student has already learned the method, do not add a generic “undo operations” or
   “choose the formula” hint merely because a hint structure was requested. Targeted feedback
   may still appear after a specific incorrect attempt.
2. **Informational hint** for a concise clue, prerequisite, or representation suggestion.
3. **Socratic checkpoint** when answering a smaller question lets the learner make the next
   decision independently. Let the learner attempt it before revealing its answer.
4. **Targeted feedback** for a plausible wrong answer or misconception. Address why that
   approach fails without revealing unrelated later work.
5. **Full solution** only after an attempt or explicit give-up. Explain the complete chain,
   including checks and an alternate method when useful.

Use as many hints as there are distinct bottlenecks—typically one to three, sometimes none,
and rarely more. Never create extra hints merely to hit a count.

## Content boundaries

- A **hint** points toward the next decision without completing it.
- A **hint answer** resolves only the smaller checkpoint posed by that hint.
- A **final answer** states the requested result without teaching the chain.
- A **full solution** explains every important move and verifies the result.
- A **prerequisite explainer** teaches reusable background knowledge and does not solve the
  present item.

Keep equations out of the first hint unless selecting or constructing that equation is the
decision being coached. Do not split substitution, arithmetic, or algebra into separate
reveals unless one of those operations is itself the learning objective.

## Multipart fading

Attach hints to Parts A, B, C, or D rather than to the problem as a whole.

- Coach representation and setup in an early part when needed.
- Let later parts reuse established results with fewer hints.
- Do not restate an earlier solution as a new hint.
- If Part C depends on Part B, allow the needed value to be revealed without exposing Part
  C's method.

Example: for a two-dimensional motion problem, Part A may ask the learner to choose axes and
draw a diagram; a later hint can reveal the labelled diagram. Part B may ask which direction
determines time. Parts C and D should not repeat the diagram or component setup unless a new
representation is required.

## Diagram rule

If choosing or drawing a diagram, graph, sign chart, table, number line, or component triangle
is an authentic first decision, make that the first Socratic prompt. Reveal the accurate,
labelled representation only after the learner considers it. Skip diagrams for basic recall,
routine algebra, and one-step substitutions.

## Interaction pattern

- Keep hints hidden until requested; opening the hint list alone reveals no content.
- Name hints by purpose, such as "Choose a representation" or "Check the domain."
- Unlock dependent hints in order; independent hints may be opened in any order.
- Preserve the learner's answer while hints open.
- In quiz/test mode, lock all hints and solutions until submission.
- After an attempted closed answer, show targeted feedback when available. For open symbolic
  or written work, use a self-scoring rubric rather than unreliable string matching.

## Validation

- Confirm variable-length sequences, including zero, one, and several hints.
- Confirm dependency unlocking and keyboard operation.
- Confirm cold/test mode contains no answer-bearing DOM content available to reveal.
- Read every hint beside the next hint and solution: remove duplication and answer leakage.
- Confirm every diagram is instructionally justified and every necessary diagram is accurate.
